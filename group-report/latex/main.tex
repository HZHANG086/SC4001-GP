\section*{Abstract}
Adapting a pretrained language model with few labels may improve classification while changing representations that support transfer. We investigate whether preserving those representations improves sentiment classification outside the labelled source domain. Seven adaptation strategies share RoBERTa-base, a linear CLS classifier, three IMDb label budgets and five paired seeds; Yelp and Amazon are held out for evaluation. We compare accuracy, representation geometry, regularization ablations, errors and computational cost. A mean-pooled feature-preservation penalty increases last-layer out-of-domain centered kernel alignment from 0.108 to 0.652 at the largest budget, yet changes accuracy by only +0.038 percentage points. Its accuracy effects vary across budgets and seeds. LoRA achieves \OODLoRAHigh\% out-of-domain accuracy at the largest budget and reduces measured allocated memory and step time relative to full tuning, but is less stable at the smallest budget and more expensive than tuning only the final two layers. These results distinguish successful representation preservation from improved transfer. The contribution is a controlled investigation of this distinction, with negative outcomes, uncertainty and reproducible evidence retained.

\section{Introduction}
A sentiment classifier trained on movie reviews may be deployed on restaurant or product reviews, where vocabulary, review length and evaluative conventions differ. Limited labelled data make adaptation choices consequential: freezing a pretrained encoder may retain generic features but constrain task learning, while unrestricted tuning can change those features. Our question is: \textbf{does preserving pretrained representations improve out-of-domain sentiment classification under limited source labels, and at what computational cost?}

We use IMDb as the only labelled training domain and evaluate unchanged models on Yelp and Amazon. The study separates freezing, partial tuning, full tuning, staged linear probing and tuning, low-rank adaptation, feature preservation and weight preservation. It tests a concrete hypothesis: anchoring a pretrained sentence representation may retain transferable information while the classifier adapts to source labels. This hypothesis requires two distinct checks: the representation must actually be preserved, and preservation must accompany improved target prediction. A successful first check does not establish the second.

Our contribution has three parts. First, we adapt a pooled cosine-preservation objective to a shared text-classification pipeline and compare it with established alternatives under fixed data, head and source-only selection rules. Second, we test its behavior through paired accuracy, layerwise geometry, penalty sweeps and error transitions. Third, we distinguish parameter efficiency from measured computational cost using matched hardware probes and separately recorded search costs. We do not claim to invent LoRA, LP-FT, L2-SP or representation regularization.

\section{Related work and positioning}
RoBERTa revisits the optimization and data choices of language-model pretraining \cite{roberta}. A common original pretrained checkpoint lets us vary adaptation without confounding the comparison with different backbones or prior sentiment fine-tuning. IMDb supplies the source task \cite{imdb}; the Yelp and Amazon polarity datasets provide external review domains \cite{reviews}.

Kumar et al. show that full fine-tuning can distort useful features under distribution shift and motivate linear probing followed by fine-tuning (LP-FT) \cite{lpft}. Their vision experiments motivate our question; they do not establish that freezing should outperform tuning in sentiment analysis. LoRA learns low-rank updates to frozen weights, providing a parameter-efficient comparator \cite{lora}. L2-SP anchors weights to their pretrained values \cite{l2sp}, whereas DELTA constrains selected convolutional feature maps using attention \cite{delta}. Our token-mean cosine penalty is a simpler adaptation of the preservation idea, not a reproduction of DELTA. CKA supplies a diagnostic of representation geometry \cite{cka}, which we interpret separately from predictive accuracy.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Shared encoder and classifier}
Let $H_\theta(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times d}$ denote the final token representations of RoBERTa-base. Every method predicts two sentiment classes using the same linear head,
\begin{equation}
 p(y\mid x)=\operatorname{softmax}(W_c H_\theta(x)_0+b_c),\qquad
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal B}\log p(y\mid x).
\end{equation}
The first-token state is referred to as CLS. There is no additional nonlinear classification head or pooler. All runs start from the same original pretrained revision, except the second stage of LP-FT, which starts from its own source-selected first-stage checkpoint.

\subsection{Feature and weight preservation}
For M6, the teacher $H_0$ is the frozen pretrained encoder in evaluation mode. Its outputs are computed without gradients on the \emph{same labelled minibatch} used for classification; no target or additional unlabelled text is used. With attention mask $m_t$, define
\begin{equation}
 z_\theta(x)=\frac{\sum_t m_t H_\theta(x)_t}{\sum_t m_t},\quad
 \mathcal R_{\mathrm{feat}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{x\in\mathcal B}
 \left(1-\frac{z_\theta(x)^\top z_0(x)}{\|z_\theta(x)\|\,\|z_0(x)\|}\right).
\end{equation}
The training objective is $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CE}}+\lambda\mathcal R_{\mathrm{feat}}$. Pooling includes special tokens and excludes padding. The student uses its training-mode forward pass. The penalty anchors the token mean, whereas the classifier reads CLS; this distinction is central to interpreting the diagnostics.

The L2-SP control uses $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CE}}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{j\in\mathrm{encoder}}(\theta_j-\theta_{0,j})^2$. It constrains all encoder parameters, excludes the classifier and uses a sum rather than a parameter-normalized mean. Its coefficient therefore cannot be compared numerically with M6's coefficient. The common AdamW weight decay remains enabled for both objectives.

\subsection{Adaptation-specific behavior}
M1 keeps the encoder in evaluation mode while learning the head. M2 enables training-mode dropout only in its final two trainable blocks. M3, M4, M6 and L2-SP fully tune the encoder. M4 first retrains a head-only model for ten epochs at the selected M1 learning rate, then searches three full-tuning rates from the best first-stage checkpoint.

M5 inserts rank-eight updates into the query and value projections of all 12 layers: $W=W_0+(\alpha/r)BA$, with $r=8$ and $\alpha=16$. The pretrained weights remain frozen, the classifier is trainable, and additional adapter dropout is zero; the encoder's original training-mode dropout remains active. The implementation uses PyTorch without an extra PEFT dependency. Adapter merging, gradients and plain-model reloads were tested before formal evaluation. Its training starts from the original pretrained model, not a B fine-tuned checkpoint.

\section{Data and experimental design}
\subsection{Source labels and target evaluation}
IMDb is the only labelled training source. Text normalization is used for duplicate detection, not model input: HTML unescaping, Unicode NFKC, line-break handling, case folding and whitespace normalization precede hashing. We remove 123 source-training records overlapping the official source test texts and 98 internal duplicates; no conflicting-label groups were found. The remaining source pool contains 24,779 reviews. Near-duplicate detection is outside this procedure.

Budgets of 400, 1,600 and 6,400 include \emph{training and validation labels}: 320/80, 1,280/320 and 5,120/1,280, respectively. Each split is balanced. Within each seed, smaller training and validation sets are separately nested within larger sets; their pools remain disjoint across budgets. Seeds 13, 21, 42, 87 and 100 are shared by all methods. Different seeds can sample overlapping texts, so they do not represent wholly independent datasets.

The official 25,000-example IMDb test set is retained unchanged, including 201 normalized duplicate extra rows. Yelp and Amazon each use 5,000 balanced examples from their official test splits, with fixed sampling seed 20261027. Target duplicates, overlap with source texts and overlap between selected target sets are removed. Amazon input concatenates title, newline and content. Target labels serve only the fixed balanced sampling and evaluation; they do not train models or select configurations. Model and dataset revisions, IDs and split hashes are archived.

\subsection{Training, selection and experimental chronology}
All candidates use ten epochs, FP32, maximum length 512, dynamic padding, batch eight and four-step gradient accumulation. AdamW uses weight decay 0.01 and warmup ratio 0.1. Source-validation accuracy selects the epoch, breaking ties in favor of the earliest checkpoint; candidate ties follow fixed list order. This is checkpoint selection over ten trained epochs, not early termination or a train-plus-validation refit.

A source-only development pilot selected length 512; B's source-only pilot informed its penalty grids. A's 90 candidate runs preceded its test access. The analyses are exploratory rather than retrospectively preregistered confirmation. Matched Ada probes support cost comparisons across all seven methods.

\section{Predictive performance and uncertainty}
\subsection{Metrics and statistical units}
The primary outcome is the equal mean of Yelp and Amazon accuracy \emph{within a seed}, followed by the five-seed mean and sample SD. IMDb measures source-domain test accuracy. Differences are paired by seed and budget. We report unadjusted 95\% paired-$t$ intervals, while recognizing that five seeds cannot establish their distributional assumptions. Exact two-sided sign-flip tests enumerate $2^5$ assignments; their minimum attainable $p$ is 0.0625. Holm correction is applied separately to three focal M6--M3 OOD comparisons, 69 original secondary contrasts and 18 later M5 contrasts. Layers, domains, examples and cost processes do not count as additional trained-model replications.

\end{center}
M2 exceeds M1's mean OOD accuracy by \MtwoMinusMoneLow, \MtwoMinusMoneMid\ and \MtwoMinusMoneHigh\ points as budgets increase. Thus some encoder adaptation helps substantially in this setting. At budget 6,400, M2 and M3 reach \OODPartialHigh\% and \OODFullHigh\%, despite very different trainable-parameter counts. This is close observed performance, not a statistical equivalence result. M3 has the highest mean at budget 400 and M4 at 6,400. At 1,600, M5 exceeds M4 by only \LoRAvsLPMid\ points; ranking rounded means is not evidence of a reliable advantage.

\begin{center}
\input{paired_table.tex}
\captionof{table}{Paired OOD differences from full fine-tuning, in percentage points. Wins count positive seedwise differences. Intervals are approximate paired-$t$ intervals; exact tests have limited resolution with five seeds.}\label{tab:paired}

M6 wins in only one, two and three seeds at the increasing budgets; its medium-budget positive mean is not a majority-seed improvement. Its exact Holm-adjusted $p$ values are 0.375, 1.000 and 1.000; all three M5--M3 values are 1.000. These results do not establish either consistent superiority or equivalence. No fixed one-percentage-point threshold is used to declare significance.

For sensitivity, 10,000 bootstrap replicates retain example pairing. Conditional fixed-model intervals omit retraining variation. At budget 400, the crossed seed/example interval for M6--M3 is $[-1.332,-0.086]$ points, while its paired-$t$ interval crosses zero. We retain this small-sample sensitivity rather than select the most favorable procedure; crossed intervals at the other budgets also span zero. A low-budget M5 seed reaches only \LoRAFailure\% OOD accuracy, and its OOD SD is \LoRASDLow\ points. Failed or unstable outcomes remain in the report.

\section{Representation preservation versus transfer}
Representation diagnostics use the same 256 deterministically selected, balanced examples from each domain for every checkpoint. We analyze the embedding output and all 12 encoder layers, both CLS states and means over non-padding tokens. For centered feature matrices $X,Y$, linear CKA is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{CKA}(X,Y)=\frac{\|X^\top Y\|_F^2}{\|X^\top X\|_F\,\|Y^\top Y\|_F}.
\end{equation}
The implementation uses centered Gram matrices with float64 accumulation after FP32 feature extraction. It also computes a debiased sensitivity estimate, a fixed input-permutation control and pointwise cosine distance. CKA describes geometry rather than semantic correctness or a causal mechanism \cite{cka}. Constant layer-0 CLS states have undefined CKA and are omitted, not scored as perfect preservation.

\fig{02_cka_layers.pdf}{1}{Layerwise OOD CKA to the pretrained encoder. Targets are averaged within each seed; thin lines show individual seeds and thick lines show means. Rows distinguish token-mean and CLS pooling. M5 shares inputs and estimators but uses Ada; B diagnostics use V100.}{fig:cka}

M6 raises last-layer mean-pooled OOD CKA from M3's 0.104, 0.095 and 0.108 to 0.274, 0.213 and 0.652. Debiased values retain the pattern (M3: 0.088, 0.080, 0.093; M6: 0.259, 0.199, 0.643), well above M6's shuffled means of 0.018, 0.015 and 0.025. The objective therefore preserves measurable geometry. Its paired accuracy effects nevertheless change sign and remain seed-sensitive (Table~\ref{tab:paired}).

The effect is pooling-dependent: M6's final CLS CKA remains close to M3's, while its objective directly anchors the token mean. A plausible interpretation is that preserving mean-pooled geometry does not sufficiently constrain the classifier's CLS feature; this explanation has not been isolated experimentally. M4 also preserves mean-pooled geometry (0.569, 0.778, 0.755) without M6's explicit penalty. L2-SP yields 0.147, 0.145 and 0.209. Hence weight preservation, staged tuning and feature preservation are not interchangeable interventions.

M5's corresponding CKA values are 0.116, 0.137 and 0.150 (debiased: 0.099, 0.118, 0.132). A small trainable-parameter fraction does not guarantee preservation of this geometry. Within-method and within-budget rank associations use five points and remain descriptive; pooling budgets would confound representation change with additional labels. Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics} provides per-domain diagnostics and seedwise plots. M1/M2 have accuracy and cost comparisons but no added CKA experiment.

\section{Regularization ablations and failure analysis}
\fig{04_lambda_ablation.pdf}{1}{Predefined penalty sweeps, with mean OOD accuracy and sample SD over five seeds. Target outcomes describe all predefined candidates; they do not replace source-only selection. The two penalty coefficient scales are not directly comparable.}{fig:ablation}

M6 selects $\lambda=0.01/0.1/1$ in 1/3/1, 4/0/1 and 1/2/2 seeds at budgets 400, 1,600 and 6,400. L2-SP selects 0.1 in 14 of 15 cells. Selected-model averages therefore mix regularization strengths. M3 is a separate $\lambda=0$ control, not a fallback in either penalty grid. At budget 6,400, M6's three-domain last-layer mean CKA ranges from roughly 0.35 to 0.99; the two seeds choosing $\lambda=1$ are near 0.98--0.99. An aggregate preservation score would conceal this heterogeneity. Strong L2-SP regularization can sharply reduce accuracy (Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}); retaining those candidates exposes the limits of the objective.

M5 likewise shows a bounded-search limitation: four of five low-budget choices use the largest predefined learning rate. Its low-budget instability is retained, and we do not expand the search after observing target results. Additional target-guided tuning would change the meaning of the comparison.

\subsection{Class, length and example-level errors}
We compare M6 and M3 on identical examples, separating shared correct predictions, shared errors, fixes and regressions. At budget 400, M6 changes Amazon accuracy by $-2.008$ points on negative examples but $+0.072$ on positive examples. At budget 1,600, changes for negative reviews are positive on both Yelp (+1.424) and Amazon (+1.104), while positive-class changes are smaller. Aggregate effects can conceal class-specific shifts; these observations do not establish the reason for them.

The length cap is another limitation. On IMDb at budget 6,400, M3 averages 92.28\% accuracy for inputs longer than 512 tokens, versus 94.79\% for lengths 129--512. These groups differ in content and difficulty, so this is not a causal estimate of truncation damage. Amazon's greater-than-512 stratum contains just one example and cannot support a reliable subgroup conclusion. Negation-marker groups use lexical heuristics, not expert linguistic annotations.

Changes at the example level are also unstable. At budget 1,600, 118 Yelp and 141 Amazon examples are fixed in at least one seed but broken in another. Only four Yelp and three Amazon examples are fixed in at least three seeds. The case file uses a fixed hash rule to select examples from each correctness-transition category at seed 13, retaining IDs, text hashes and short excerpts. Appendix~\ref{app:repro} shows a fixed-budget fix/regression pair. It illustrates recorded predictions, not a proven semantic mechanism. AI-assisted case interpretations are not treated as human-coded evidence.

\section{Computational cost and practical trade-offs}
\subsection{What the measurements cover}
We separate trainable parameters, complete candidate-training records, search, short computational probes and inference. A/B training used V100; M5 used RTX 6000 Ada. Raw times across these GPUs are not treated as method speedups. To compare methods on one platform, we ran matched Ada training probes for all seven methods. Each uses the same 32 source-training examples, tokenized and resident on the GPU, length 512, batch eight, accumulation four and FP32 with TF32 disabled. Two optimizer steps warm up each process and five steps are measured. Three fresh processes estimate timing variation; these are not additional training seeds.

\fig{06_ada_cost.pdf}{1}{Matched RTX 6000 Ada training probes, three fresh processes per method. M4 represents its second stage only. M6 retains the teacher required by its loss; L2-SP retains its pretrained-weight snapshot. Memory is peak allocated tensor memory, not total device occupancy or a guaranteed provisioning requirement.}{fig:cost}

M3 uses \FullMemory\ GiB and \FullStep\ seconds per optimizer step; M5 uses \LoRAMemory\ GiB and \LoRAStep\ seconds, reductions of \MemorySaving\% and \TimeSaving\%. Its 0.24\% trainable-parameter fraction does not imply proportionally low compute. M1 and M2 remain cheaper: \ProbeMemory\ and \PartialMemory\ GiB, with \ProbeStep\ and \PartialStep\ seconds per step. M5 consumes \MemoryVsPartial\% more memory and \TimeVsPartial\% more step time than M2; its budget-6,400 mean OOD advantage is only 0.176 points, with paired interval $[-0.394,0.746]$. LoRA saves optimizer state, but adapters in all layers still require backward computation.

M6 uses \FeatureMemory\ GiB and \FeatureStep\ seconds, while L2-SP uses \WeightMemory\ GiB and \WeightStep\ seconds. Neither adds a stable accuracy advantage over M3. In the original B implementation a diagnostic teacher was retained even for losses that do not require it. Clean V100 probes show that this adds about 0.472 GiB for M3/M4; it is separated from method-essential cost. Appendix~\ref{app:repro} retains V100 measurements as a distinct hardware stratum.

\subsection{Search, interruptions and deployment boundaries}
Recorded candidate time includes training, epoch validation and checkpoint saving, excluding outer-process startup, model loading and data mapping. M4 additionally incurs its first stage and inherited M1 search; standalone M6/L2-SP reproduction requires inherited M3 search. Shared upstream searches should not be counted repeatedly in the actual project total. One nonselected M6 run (budget 6,400, seed 21, $\lambda=1$) resumed after timeout; its recorded time covers only its final invocation, so that cell's search total is a lower bound. Its selected $\lambda=0.01$ run did not resume.

All 45 M5 candidate invocations completed without resume and sum to \LoRATrainingHours\ recorded hours on Ada, excluding formal testing and diagnostics. The archived inference probes use a fresh pretrained encoder and seeded classifier rather than trained winners; the LoRA probe is unmerged. They characterize an implementation at a fixed shape, not trained-model deployment throughput. We make no deployment-speed or cross-hardware speedup claim.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
The central hypothesis separates into preservation and prediction. The feature objective successfully increases mean-pooled similarity to pretraining, yet paired target-accuracy effects remain mixed. This finding supports a conditional interpretation of preservation: geometry can be retained without providing the decision features needed for better transfer. The mismatch between the penalized mean representation and the CLS classifier is a plausible contributor, but a new controlled intervention would be needed to establish causality.

M1's weaker performance shows that retaining the encoder unchanged is not sufficient in this setting. M2 offers a strong practical trade-off at the largest budget: its observed OOD accuracy is close to full tuning with substantially less measured compute. LoRA also reduces measured resources relative to full tuning, but its benefit depends on the comparator and budget. Choosing an adaptation strategy from parameter count or the single highest mean would ignore instability and implementation costs.

The study is limited to one encoder family, one source and two fixed review-domain targets. Balanced target subsets do not represent naturally imbalanced deployment traffic, and absolute accuracies across different datasets do not directly measure shift severity. Five paired seeds share a source pool and test sets; their intervals and exact tests have limited resolution. The 512-token cap and duplicate IMDb test rows affect interpretation. CKA depends on pooling, layer and a 256-example subset, and it does not establish semantic preservation. M5 diagnostics use Ada while B uses V100; inputs, estimators and precision match, but small numerical differences remain possible. Candidate grids are bounded and inherited searches make budgets unequal. Later analyses and M5 design were informed by A/B results, although selection remained source-only. None of these observations justifies retuning on the target sets.

\section{Conclusion}
Under this controlled low-label sentiment protocol, preserving pretrained mean-pooled representations does not reliably improve cross-domain accuracy. Feature regularization, weight anchoring, staged adaptation and LoRA produce different geometric and computational effects; their ordering is not captured by one preservation score. The useful outcome is a measured trade-off and a supported negative result: evaluate target generalization and resource use directly, preserve paired uncertainty, and do not assume that smaller representational change or fewer trainable parameters guarantees a better method.
